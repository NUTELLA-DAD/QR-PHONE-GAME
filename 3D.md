# Airship Crew: the 3D transition (master plan)

Planned by Fable after reading the docs, the 3D test page (`public/three3d.html`, `public/modules/three3d/`) and the simulation modules. Lead's notes are marked **Lead:**.

**Lead:**
- The owner asked for this on 2026-10-10: "go all out", a AAA-looking 3D game, more realistic physics and collision, and specific ship parts breaking visibly in 3D, without busting usage limits.
- Rapier 0.20.0 (deterministic-compat) and Three.js r169 are bundled in `public/vendor/`.
- **Owner decision pending:** Fable recommends the HYBRID physics in section 3. The game's own 2D physics stays in charge of gameplay, and Rapier runs the 3D wreckage. Moving the ships themselves onto Rapier is possible, but it would throw away the test baselines and need a full re-tune.

## Facts that decide the plan
1. Rapier is already bundled (`public/vendor/rapier/rapier.mjs`, 2.9 MB). No physics download is needed.
2. The SDXL pipeline is not usable today: ComfyUI Desktop is installed but has no model files. So textures are procedural canvas paintings; an SDXL texture pass would need a ~7 GB download and the owner's OK.
3. The dev laptop has an RTX 5070 Ti, but venue laptops will be weaker, so quality tiers are mandatory.
4. The Three.js post-processing add-ons are in `node_modules` (copying them in is not a download).
5. `render.js renderFrame(time, view, { layers: ['hud','arrows'] })` already draws only the HUD, and `camera.js` gives the view that the 3D camera follows. So the HUD stays canvas 2D on top of the WebGL canvas, with no HUD rewrite.
6. Per-part damage already lives in the simulation: `modules.js` hp/broken, `gasHoles`, `fires`, `breaches`, scars, debris pieces, `ramHits`. 3D destruction is mostly a *view* of that state, plus a small event log.
7. The step order and random-number order are sacred (byte-identical botsim baseline). Simulation additions for 3D must only add data.
8. `pngjs` is in `node_modules`, so screenshot-diff gates need no new package.

## 1. Look: a painted storybook, built as a model and lit with real lamps
**References:**
- *Sea of Thieves*: water and lantern-lit interiors.
- *Wind Waker* and *Sable*: cel shading and steady ink lines.
- *Tunic*: per-world colour grading.
- *Lovers in a Dangerous Spacetime*: sofa readability.

**Materials, "gouache cel":**
- `MeshToonMaterial` with a 3-step gradient, procedural painted textures, vertex-colour AO in rooms, and a thin warm rim.
- No `MeshStandardMaterial` on gameplay objects, because it reads as a "toy diorama".
- Backgrounds stay unlit painted planes.

**Ink:** inverted-hull outlines in three widths. No screen-space edges on ships, because they swim.

**Post (one EffectComposer):**
- Render, then bloom (half-res, emissives only: lanterns, fire, muzzle, eyes).
- Then a per-environment LUT made from `config.ENVIRONMENTS.*.TINT`.
- Then a finish pass: vignette, static paper grain, slight shadow desaturation.
- NeutralToneMapping.
- No SSAO, and no depth of field in play (only on finale freezes).
- God rays only at dusk on High. Fog per environment.

**Lights per environment:**
- One warm key light (Ember lights from below) plus a hemisphere light; only the key casts shadows.
- Fungal and Storm have no key light: lanterns, searchlights and lightning light them.
- Lantern point lights are capped at 6 per ship on High, 2 on Medium, 0 on Low.

**Searchlights:** a real SpotLight plus one soft volumetric cone (depth-faded) and a light disc where the beam hits rock or water.

**Water:** a flat toon plane with stepped foam bands, a shoreline foam ring, splashes and a cheap fake reflection. NO wave displacement, because that would be wobble.

**Clouds and particles:**
- Clouds are instanced billboard clusters.
- Particles are one GPU instanced system: fire as an 8 fps flipbook, smoke/steam, sparks, splinters.

**No-wobble rules (binding on every package):**
1. No idle sine except the ship's bob (±3 px).
2. Secondary motion uses stepped 8 fps keys.
3. Outlines are geometry.
4. The camera never rolls or breathes; shake is a 3-step translational kick.
5. No vertex displacement (water, cloth, bags); bag swell is a scale.
6. Particles move linearly or by gravity, never on noise curls.

## 2. Assets with no internet and no artist
- **Procedural ships:** the ship is built from its parts list like LEGO. `view3d/parts3d/registry.js` maps every part kind to a builder: hull (lofted gondola, chines, planking, rudder), decks, bag (lathe envelope, ribs, patches, catenary rigging), stations, guns, engines and fittings.
  - Every sub-mesh is **keyed by part id**, so destruction can detach exactly what `breakOff.js` cuts.
  - The fallback list must be empty (a gate).
- **Textures:** one 2048² trim sheet painted by code at load (about 40 ms) holds planks, canvas, brass, iron, rope, rock strata, foam, clouds, fire flipbook, smoke, and scorch/hole decals. The existing `art/textures/*.png` can be blended in as grain.
- **Decals:** instanced, up to 64 per ship, from the hit log.
- **LOD:** three detail levels by quality tier, plus a silhouette model for far ships.
- **Crew:** a distinct head per species, hats, goggles, scarves and carried items. Animation uses stepped pose keys: a 4-key walk at 8 fps, a crouch before a jump, a landing squash, and a tipped knock-out pose.
- **The Kraken:** a lathe mantle with a crest, mottled skin, instanced suckers, glowing eyes and beak, and rigid segments from the sim. Severed chunks become Rapier bodies. The same kit serves the other creatures.
- **Enemies:** gunships use the ship builder with the enemy theme. Planes and bats get a detail pass. Mines, turrets, outposts, the Flagship and wrecks follow.

## 3. Physics and collision: HYBRID (recommended)
**Gameplay stays plane-locked in the custom simulation**, which is tested, deterministic and tuned. **Rapier runs a 3D "wreckage world" in the renderer:**
- broken-off parts, severed tentacles, thrown cargo, wreck pieces;
- terrain trimesh, a water plane with buoyancy, and the ships as kinematic compound boxes, so debris bounces off the hull it just left;
- a fixed 60 Hz step, stable insertion order, and a seeded scatter;
- `tools/physics-check.mjs` asserts the same hash twice.

**More exact collisions in the game itself (authoritative, byte-identical-safe):**
- `shipBuild.js` gains `partRects`, one box per part.
- `impact`, `crash`, ram and rock contacts append `{t, x, y, power, partId, kind}` to a per-ship `hitLog` ring buffer, so the hit you see is the hit that counts.
- None of this draws random numbers or reorders steps.

Why not Rapier for the ships themselves:
- every gate baseline would be thrown away and the game re-tuned;
- crew walking, towing, Kraken grips and engine forces are all built on ship space;
- the tools would all need WASM.

## 4. Destruction you can see
| Simulation state | 3D look |
|---|---|
| `modules[name].hp/broken` | 4 states: intact, scuffed, damaged (dent, hanging part, smoke), broken (bent barrel, stopped prop, dark boiler, torn sail) |
| `hitLog` | dent/scorch decals, splinters on wood, sparks on iron, a flash |
| `gasHoles` | rip decals; patched holes show stitches; a lost bag deflates and crumples (scale plus a sag key) |
| `fires` | flames and a smoke column; a scorch mark that stays after the fire is out |
| `breaches` | a hull hole with planks sticking out |
| `ramHits` | prow dents |
| hull share | soot and scorch tint as the hull drops |
| scars | ragged, charred plank rims round the hole |

**Break-off:**
- `breakOff` pushes `{plan, origin, blast, cause, t}` to a `breakEvents` key BEFORE applying the layout.
- The view detaches the keyed sub-meshes of the lost parts into Rapier bodies with the debris velocities plus the blast kick, adds smoke and fire, then rebuilds the ship (scar rims show).
- Pieces bounce on rock, splash and float or sink, and fade when the 2D debris expires.
- Meshes are pre-fractured by part (the parts list is the fracture pattern). Blasts add plank and iron chunks as particles.
- A "hanging" damaged part is a pose, not a joint.

Gameplay consequences already exist: a lost engine means less thrust, a lost bag means less lift, a lost coal bunker starves the boiler. The view reads the per-engine throttles to spin each prop.

## 5. Switching the TV to 3D
- New folder `public/modules/view3d/`; the test page's files move there and grow. `createView3D({canvas, hud, state, camera2d})` follows the same `renderFrame` contract.
- `host.html` gets a `<canvas id="c3d">` under the 2D canvas. The 2D canvas draws only the HUD, arrows and full-screen cards.
- Choosing the view:
  - `?view=2d|3d`, then `localStorage`, then a quality probe;
  - the pause menu gets "View: 3D/2D";
  - any WebGL failure falls back to 2D.
- Camera:
  - `camera.js` stays the authority; a perspective camera maps the gameplay plane 1:1, at elevation 0.12 rad;
  - HUD anchors use `worldToScreen`;
  - cinematic moments, never with roll: a come-about dolly, a breach pull-back, a finale orbit;
  - the Versus porthole becomes a render target.
- The build page gets a 3D toggle with orbit; the blueprint stays 2D ink.
- Phones are unchanged.
- Everything stays offline: `public/vendor/` is committed, and `start.bat` is untouched.

## 6. Performance
**Target (Medium tier, Intel UHD 620 class, 1080p):** 60 fps with 2 ships, the Kraken and 200 particles; at most 120 draw calls, 350k triangles and 4 ms view JS.

**Tiers:**

| Tier | What it adds or drops |
|---|---|
| High | spot shadows, 6 lanterns, god rays, more ink, shadows 4096 |
| Medium | the target above |
| Low | plain lit, no shadows, LUT only, pixel ratio 0.75 |
| 2D | today's renderer |

**Auto-detect:** probe the GPU, then the existing `perf.js` governor steps down the tiers.

**Perf gate:** `--check-3d` fails above 150 draw calls, 450k triangles or 6 ms view JS.

## 10. WP2 notes: ship materials and hull v2 (built)
**Files** (`public/modules/view3d/`): `textures.js` (the 2048² trim sheet), `parts3d/kit.js` (pieces, trims, the assembler), `parts3d/registry.js` (kind -> builder), `hull.js`, `bag.js`, `decks.js`, `rooms.js`, `stations.js`, `weapons.js`, `engines.js`. `shipMesh.js` is now only the orchestrator and the per-frame `update`.

**Trim sheet.** Painted with Canvas 2D on the first ship (about 13 ms; the old `art/textures/*.png` are blended in as a faint grain when they load). The rows are modulators (near white, grey), the vertex colours carry the hue, so the enemy theme reuses it. Rects: `TRIM` (pixels), `uvRect(name)`, `uvAt(name, tx, ty)`, `plankLayout(name)` (where the boards and butt joints are). WP3/WP4 already have their rows: `rock0-2`, `foam`, `cloud0-7`, `fire0-3`, `smoke`, `scorch0-2`, `hole0-3` (all transparent where they should be). Use `getTrimSheet().texture` (one texture, anisotropy 4, mipmaps). Do not paint a second sheet.

**Registry and keys (what WP5 needs).** `REGISTRY[kind].build(part, ctx, arg) -> { key, batches, dyn, bounds }` for every kind in `shipBuild.PARTS` (data-only kinds have `art: false`). Part ids: `hull`, `deck:<id>`, `room:<name>`, `<type>:<n>` for ladders/ropes/stairs/lifts/poles, `station:<name>`, `gun:<name>`, `searchlight:<name>`, `engine:<name>`, `sail:<name>`, `cannon:<name>`, `gasbag:bagN`, `pipe:*`, `rack:*`, `dropHatch:<name>`, `armour:*`, `ramProw`, `coil`, `medbay` ... The assembler (`kit.assemble`) merges every part's pieces into ONE geometry (`model.assembled`) and keeps `model.ranges[key] = [{ layer, start, count }]` (vertex ranges; the geometry holds each piece twice: the picture and its ink shell, so a ship is one draw call). `model.parts.get(key) = { key, kind, name, deck, x, x0, x1, dyn, bounds }` says what a part is and where. To break a part off: `model.extractPart(key)` (a Group of just its triangles, ink included) plus `parts.get(key).dyn` nodes (guns, props, lamps, hatch leaves, the bag's group), then rebuild the ship from the new layout (the view already does that when `layout.version` changes). Plank and canvas uv offsets are seeded from the part key, so a rebuild does not shuffle them.

**Cut-away.** The hull's two walls are the `neg` and `pos` layers; the wall facing the camera is hidden by a shader switch (`uHide`, one value per ship material, set by `model.setView`) in the picture pass and in the shadow pass. No vertex wobble anywhere: the bag swell is still a scale of its group; the prop angle only integrates `state.engines[i].pow` (read only).

**Measured (classic, day, one ship incl. one crewman):** 81 -> 43 draw calls, 87k -> 101k triangles (mesh + ink + shadow pass); 39 k unique triangles; trim sheet 13 ms; a ship build about 40 ms.

## 11. WP3 notes: terrain, water, clouds, dark caves (built)
**Files** (`public/modules/view3d/`): `terrain.js` (rewritten), `water.js` and `clouds.js` (new), `world.js` (ties the stage together), `lights.js` (the darkness), `sky.js` (the seam fix), `style.js`/`quality.js` (3 new kill-switches), `config.LOOK3D.<env>` (`dark`, `cap`, `clouds`, `water`; see the comment above `LOOK3D`).

**Kill-switches:** `?look=nodark` (the old day-lit caves), `?look=noclouds`, `?look=nowater` (the old plain flat water, no foam, shore or splashes). The dev page has buttons for all three.

**The cave seam (found).** The cave backdrop plane used to RECEIVE the key light's shadows. The key's shadow camera is a box (fitted to the ships, snapped to 200 units): outside it there is no shadow, inside it the rock slab and the ship shade the picture, so the backdrop jumped in brightness along the frustum's top / bottom edge. A horizontal line at about 40% of the screen, a step of 20 -> 29 grey levels in one row (measured on `wp1_after_cave.png`); the same shadows also drew the dark blocks and vertical edges on the picture (`wp3_before_cave.png`). Fix: `cavePlane.receiveShadow = false` (the lamps' light still lands on it).

**Terrain.** Same marching-squares footprint as the 2D game (collision silhouette untouched: only DEPTH is displaced, never x/y). Squares near a cut edge are cut into 3x3 little squares (clipped to the outline); far squares are one flat quad each. Relief = two octaves of stable value noise (integer hash of the world position, so a chunk is the same whenever it is built and neighbours join without cracks), fading to a flat slab 200 px from the edges; the lip is bevelled (rolls back 82 units, width 52-90 by hash); strata = the trim sheet's `rock0-2` rows blended per fragment by a continuous strata coordinate (a small shader patch on the toon material, `aRockUv` + `aStrata` attributes); mossy / snowy caps by environment (vertex colour + a small puff, on up-facing edges and tunnel floors); an ink ribbon along every lip that follows the displaced lip; tunnel walls are rows in depth with hashed bulges, shaded as their plane (a wall is seen edge-on, so per-facet normals made a mosaic of toon steps and rim light). The rock has its own 4-step toon gradient with a real dark step (a lamp behind the front face does not shine through it). Open-sky rock still casts no shadows; cave rock can. Budget kept: 2 chunks per frame.

**Dark caves (approach).** The game's `darkTarget` already drives `world.setTod` (dusk, storm, fungal, aether, ...). 3D adds one number per environment: `LOOK3D.<env>.dark.cave`, the darkness a cave map always has (2D `SEARCHLIGHT.DARK.CAVE` is 0 and stays 0: the sim's bots read it; the owner wants 3D caves dark). `lights.apply` takes the effective darkness `n = max(tod.night, cave floor)`, derives a gloom 0..1 (`dark.from`..`dark.to`) and, with it: the hemisphere light goes to `dark.hemi` with a dark-blue sky/ground floor colour, the key light to `dark.key` of itself, the thin rim fades out, the rock keeps a faint dark-blue floor (`fx.uFloor`, so a dark rock still reads as a silhouette), the cave picture's own glow drops. `world.night` = the effective darkness, so the ship's lamps, lanterns, boiler light and searchlights switch on in caves exactly as they do at night. The lamp lights are what you see by. Tiers: Medium has fewer real lights, so each reaches 2.4x further (the decks stay readable); Low has none, so its gloom is halved. Compared with 2D: the same places are dark (dark skies, dusk) plus the caves.

**Water (Sunken Sea).** One opaque plane at the sea level (it still hides the Kraken's segments below the line). Two colour steps by depth (world z), a stepped mirrored-sky gradient on Medium/High, foam lanes from the trim sheet's foam row (alpha cut in two steps) sliding at a constant speed per lane (linear in time), a hazy edge, a shoreline ring (shallow band + foam ribbon) built once per map where the rock meets the water. `world.splashAt(x, y, size, z)` (`water.splashAt`) draws a double ring decal on the surface for the VFX pass (24 rings, 1 s, quantised to 8 fps, linear growth). Colours and speed: `LOOK3D.sea.water`.

**Clouds.** One InstancedMesh (one draw call): clusters of 3-7 discs from the cloud atlas, drifting at a constant speed in the vertex shader and wrapped round the camera far off screen. Most stand behind the gameplay plane (z -1500..-3800), a few in front (z +350..+1100), thin and faded toward the screen's middle so they never cover the ship for long. Per environment `LOOK3D.<env>.clouds { n, front, alpha, tint }`; none in caves.

**Measured (Medium, 1080p headless, `shot3d`).** Chunk build: median 0.5 ms, p95 0.9 ms, max 1.1 ms once warm (the very first chunks, cold JIT, 3-5 ms); 35 chunks = 13-16k triangles (was 2-8k). Water + clouds together: +3 draw calls, +about 200 triangles (the kill-switches, same frame). In a dark cave on High the first ship's searchlight casts a shadow (a second shadow pass, +30-45k triangles); Medium does not. Host Medium before -> after: day 100 -> 102 calls / 91k -> 94k tris, cave 122 -> 119 / 96k -> 103k, Versus 136 -> 141 / 224k -> 227k. Sim gates: `--check-botsim` byte-identical, `--lint` pass (no simulation file touched).

**For WP4 / later.** `world.splashAt` is the splash hook. `terrain.stats` = chunk times. The shore ring and the splash rings are plain decals: WP4's particles can sit on top. `shipMesh.js` line 200 (the flame frame index `Math.floor(t * 8 + fr.x * 0.013) % 4` can go negative or NaN, so `[fi][ti]` throws "Cannot read properties of undefined" once with a fire on a deck, logged as a warning, not an error): left alone (not mine), needs `((fi % 4) + 4) % 4`.

## 12. WP4 notes: the VFX pass, GPU particles (built)
**Files** (`public/modules/view3d/`): `particles.js` (the system), `vfx.js` (the emitters that read the game's state), `config.VFX3D` (caps, rates, colours), small edits in `index.js` (creates it, one `vfx.update` a frame after the ships and the Kraken are placed), `shipMesh.js` (the old three-tongue flame cones only run when `model.flameFallback` is set), `quality.js` / `style.js` (`?look=novfx` kill-switch: the old flame cones and puff balls come back), the dev page (`VFX test (V)` button, a `Particles` toggle, a particle line in the HUD). No simulation file is touched (`--check-botsim` byte-identical, `--lint` pass).

**The system.** One fixed pool in a `Float32Array`, simulated in JS (linear motion, or plain gravity; drag only slows sideways; no noise anywhere), written once a frame into two interleaved instance buffers and uploaded once. Caps by tier (`config.VFX3D.CAP`): High 1500, Medium 800, Low 400; splinter boxes 160 / 90 / 0; Low also has no smoke trails and half the emit rate (`RATE`). When the pool is full new particles are dropped, smoke first (above 82%), then sparks (94%). Draw calls: **2** quad meshes + **1** instanced mesh of small lit boxes (splinters and chips, toon material, constant spin, gravity) = 3 at most.
- *alpha mesh* (smoke, steam, dust, water droplets): the trim sheet's `smoke` disc (rim softened in the shader), tinted by the hemisphere light (`setAmbient`), so it goes dark at night; fresh smoke is lit orange by its fire for a moment (`warm`). It ignores depth, because a ship's bag and decks would otherwise swallow a column rising from a room.
- *additive mesh* (fire, sparks, embers, muzzle star, rings, flashes, fireballs): HDR colours, so the core of a flame crosses the bloom threshold (keep the HDR near 1.7: more and the tone mapping bleaches it pink). Blending is "premultiplied": a flame covers a bright sky a little instead of washing out. Fire = the 4-frame flipbook stepped at 8 fps; the muzzle flash = a 2-frame star (6 rays, then 8 turned half a step); rings grow in steps at 8 fps; sparks are stretched along their velocity; `ball` is a round toon fireball in three flat colour steps with lumps fixed per particle (no animated noise).
- The random numbers are `particles.rnd` (its own seeded generator): the view never draws from `Math.random`, which the simulation shares in the browser.
- Spawns outside the view (the camera's look-at box plus a margin) are skipped.

**Emitters** (`vfx.js`, all read-only): `state.fires` of EVERY ship (flame flipbook sprite that sits on the deck, little rising flames, embers, a smoke column, a warm glow disc that is strong at night); `puffs` (grouped by place and colour and told apart by colour: orange = fireballs + sparks + smoke, + splinters when it is on a hull; gold = sparks; grey = smoke; white = steam; brown = dust + chips; cold = spray + a water ring on the sea); `flashes` (the muzzle star; the flamethrower's glow flashes become little flames); `rings` (impact ring; at `HITSTOP_AT` 190 world units or more also the one-frame bright flash + sparks); `shells` and `bullets` (a tracer streak, smoke trail on High / Medium, a muzzle star where a NEW shell appears, with the sim's own flash de-duplicated); bombs (smoke + embers trail); the boiler (chimney steam by pressure, thicker and darker in overdrive; vent plumes when a valve is open, a hiss above the warning pressure); engines (exhaust by each engine's own `pow`); the Kraken (spray + mist + `world.splashAt` where a segment crosses the sea line, bigger for the mantle, spray and chips where a tentacle grips); burning debris; the lightning coil's bolt (a bright streak between the bolt's points + a flash). The first frame (or a gap over 0.6 s) only learns what is already there, so a resumed tab does not replay old events.

**API for WP5** (`view.vfx` = `createVfx`, and `view.vfx.P` = the particle system; coordinates are the 3D world's: x = the game's x, **y = UP (the game's y negated)**, z toward the viewer, gameplay plane z = 0; sizes in world units):
- `P.burst(kind, x, y, z, n, opts)` with kind `'fire' | 'smoke' | 'steam' | 'dust' | 'spark' | 'ember' | 'drop' | 'ball' | 'flash' | 'ring' | 'muzzle'`. opts: `dir` (radians, 0 = +x, PI/2 = up, the default), `spread` (half-angle, default PI = all round), `speed`, `life`, `size` ([min, max] or a number), `size1` (end size as a multiple), `color` (hex), `vx vy vz` (inherited velocity), `up`, `ay` (acceleration, negative = down), `drag`, `alpha`, `hdr`, `stretch`, `warm`, `area` (scatter), `force` (spawn even off screen). Returns how many were made.
- `P.spawn(kind, x, y, z, opts)` one particle; `P.splinters(x, y, z, n, { kind: 'wood' | 'iron' | 'rock', speed, up, size, vx, vy, dir, spread })`; `P.explosion(x, y, z, size = 1, { wood, smoke })` the prefab (1 = a shell hit, 2-3 = a bomb or a crash); `P.flash`, `P.ring(x, y, z, size, color, life)`, `P.muzzle(x, y, z, angle, size, color)`, `P.hitFlash(x, y, z, size)`; `P.sprite(...)` one-frame immediate quads (call between `P.begin()` and `P.update()`: only the emitters need it).
- For a broken-off part: `P.explosion(x, y, z, 1.4, { wood: true })` at the break, `P.burst('fire', ...)` + `P.burst('smoke', ...)` on the falling piece each frame (the dev `demo` lists in `vfx.js` show the pattern), `P.splinters(...)` where it lands. The pool is shared: cap the calls per frame (a burst stops by itself when the pool is full).

**Measured** (Medium, 1080p, headless Chrome on the RTX 5070 Ti, `shot3d --gl default`): the pool step + buffer write for **800 live particles = 0.02 ms a frame** (1000-frame loop); the emitters + that = about 0.1 ms in the view's own meter (its timer is coarse). Scene draw calls with fire, fighters, a gunship and the test volley: 176 with the particles, 176 with `?look=novfx` (the old cones and balls cost about what the 2 particle calls do). fps stays at the display's limit (about 170) with 800 live particles. Caveats: the dev page's 8 bots put a fire out in a few seconds, so deck-fire screenshots use the `VFX test` fires (they ride the ship like real ones). Lightning, steam and splinters are the least tuned.
