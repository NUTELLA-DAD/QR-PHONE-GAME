# Airship Crew: the 3D transition (master plan)

Planned by Fable after reading the docs, the 3D test page (`public/three3d.html`, `public/modules/three3d/`) and the simulation modules. Lead's notes are marked **Lead:**.

**Lead:**
- The owner asked for this on 2026-10-10: "go all out", a AAA-looking 3D game, more realistic physics and collision, and specific ship parts breaking visibly in 3D, without busting usage limits.
- Rapier 0.20.0 (deterministic-compat) and Three.js r169 are bundled in `public/vendor/`.
- **Owner decision pending:** Fable recommends the HYBRID physics in section 3. The game's own 2D physics stays in charge of gameplay, and Rapier runs the 3D wreckage. Moving the ships themselves onto Rapier is possible, but it would throw away the test baselines and need a full re-tune.

## Facts that decide the plan
1. Rapier is already bundled (`public/vendor/rapier/rapier.mjs`, 2.9 MB). No physics download is needed.
2. The SDXL pipeline is not usable today: ComfyUI Desktop is installed but has no model files. So textures are procedural canvas paintings; an SDXL texture pass would need a ~7 GB download and the owner's OK.
3. The dev laptop has an RTX 5070 Ti, but venue laptops will be weaker, so quality tiers are mandatory.
4. The Three.js post-processing add-ons are in `node_modules` (copying them in is not a download).
5. `render.js renderFrame(time, view, { layers: ['hud','arrows'] })` already draws only the HUD, and `camera.js` gives the view that the 3D camera follows. So the HUD stays canvas 2D on top of the WebGL canvas, with no HUD rewrite.
6. Per-part damage already lives in the simulation: `modules.js` hp/broken, `gasHoles`, `fires`, `breaches`, scars, debris pieces, `ramHits`. 3D destruction is mostly a *view* of that state, plus a small event log.
7. The step order and random-number order are sacred (byte-identical botsim baseline). Simulation additions for 3D must only add data.
8. `pngjs` is in `node_modules`, so screenshot-diff gates need no new package.

## 1. Look: a painted storybook, built as a model and lit with real lamps
**References:**
- *Sea of Thieves*: water and lantern-lit interiors.
- *Wind Waker* and *Sable*: cel shading and steady ink lines.
- *Tunic*: per-world colour grading.
- *Lovers in a Dangerous Spacetime*: sofa readability.

**Materials, "gouache cel":**
- `MeshToonMaterial` with a 3-step gradient, procedural painted textures, vertex-colour AO in rooms, and a thin warm rim.
- No `MeshStandardMaterial` on gameplay objects, because it reads as a "toy diorama".
- Backgrounds stay unlit painted planes.

**Ink:** inverted-hull outlines in three widths. No screen-space edges on ships, because they swim.

**Post (one EffectComposer):**
- Render, then bloom (half-res, emissives only: lanterns, fire, muzzle, eyes).
- Then a per-environment LUT made from `config.ENVIRONMENTS.*.TINT`.
- Then a finish pass: vignette, static paper grain, slight shadow desaturation.
- NeutralToneMapping.
- No SSAO, and no depth of field in play (only on finale freezes).
- God rays only at dusk on High. Fog per environment.

**Lights per environment:**
- One warm key light (Ember lights from below) plus a hemisphere light; only the key casts shadows.
- Fungal and Storm have no key light: lanterns, searchlights and lightning light them.
- Lantern point lights are capped at 6 per ship on High, 2 on Medium, 0 on Low.

**Searchlights:** a real SpotLight plus one soft volumetric cone (depth-faded) and a light disc where the beam hits rock or water.

**Water:** a flat toon plane with stepped foam bands, a shoreline foam ring, splashes and a cheap fake reflection. NO wave displacement, because that would be wobble.

**Clouds and particles:**
- Clouds are instanced billboard clusters.
- Particles are one GPU instanced system: fire as an 8 fps flipbook, smoke/steam, sparks, splinters.

**No-wobble rules (binding on every package):**
1. No idle sine except the ship's bob (±3 px).
2. Secondary motion uses stepped 8 fps keys.
3. Outlines are geometry.
4. The camera never rolls or breathes; shake is a 3-step translational kick.
5. No vertex displacement (water, cloth, bags); bag swell is a scale.
6. Particles move linearly or by gravity, never on noise curls.

## 2. Assets with no internet and no artist
- **Procedural ships:** the ship is built from its parts list like LEGO. `view3d/parts3d/registry.js` maps every part kind to a builder: hull (lofted gondola, chines, planking, rudder), decks, bag (lathe envelope, ribs, patches, catenary rigging), stations, guns, engines and fittings.
  - Every sub-mesh is **keyed by part id**, so destruction can detach exactly what `breakOff.js` cuts.
  - The fallback list must be empty (a gate).
- **Textures:** one 2048² trim sheet painted by code at load (about 40 ms) holds planks, canvas, brass, iron, rope, rock strata, foam, clouds, fire flipbook, smoke, and scorch/hole decals. The existing `art/textures/*.png` can be blended in as grain.
- **Decals:** instanced, up to 64 per ship, from the hit log.
- **LOD:** three detail levels by quality tier, plus a silhouette model for far ships.
- **Crew:** a distinct head per species, hats, goggles, scarves and carried items. Animation uses stepped pose keys: a 4-key walk at 8 fps, a crouch before a jump, a landing squash, and a tipped knock-out pose.
- **The Kraken:** a lathe mantle with a crest, mottled skin, instanced suckers, glowing eyes and beak, and rigid segments from the sim. Severed chunks become Rapier bodies. The same kit serves the other creatures.
- **Enemies:** gunships use the ship builder with the enemy theme. Planes and bats get a detail pass. Mines, turrets, outposts, the Flagship and wrecks follow.

## 3. Physics and collision: HYBRID (recommended)
**Gameplay stays plane-locked in the custom simulation**, which is tested, deterministic and tuned. **Rapier runs a 3D "wreckage world" in the renderer:**
- broken-off parts, severed tentacles, thrown cargo, wreck pieces;
- terrain trimesh, a water plane with buoyancy, and the ships as kinematic compound boxes, so debris bounces off the hull it just left;
- a fixed 60 Hz step, stable insertion order, and a seeded scatter;
- `tools/physics-check.mjs` asserts the same hash twice.

**More exact collisions in the game itself (authoritative, byte-identical-safe):**
- `shipBuild.js` gains `partRects`, one box per part.
- `impact`, `crash`, ram and rock contacts append `{t, x, y, power, partId, kind}` to a per-ship `hitLog` ring buffer, so the hit you see is the hit that counts.
- None of this draws random numbers or reorders steps.

Why not Rapier for the ships themselves:
- every gate baseline would be thrown away and the game re-tuned;
- crew walking, towing, Kraken grips and engine forces are all built on ship space;
- the tools would all need WASM.

## 4. Destruction you can see
| Simulation state | 3D look |
|---|---|
| `modules[name].hp/broken` | 4 states: intact, scuffed, damaged (dent, hanging part, smoke), broken (bent barrel, stopped prop, dark boiler, torn sail) |
| `hitLog` | dent/scorch decals, splinters on wood, sparks on iron, a flash |
| `gasHoles` | rip decals; patched holes show stitches; a lost bag deflates and crumples (scale plus a sag key) |
| `fires` | flames and a smoke column; a scorch mark that stays after the fire is out |
| `breaches` | a hull hole with planks sticking out |
| `ramHits` | prow dents |
| hull share | soot and scorch tint as the hull drops |
| scars | ragged, charred plank rims round the hole |

**Break-off:**
- `breakOff` pushes `{plan, origin, blast, cause, t}` to a `breakEvents` key BEFORE applying the layout.
- The view detaches the keyed sub-meshes of the lost parts into Rapier bodies with the debris velocities plus the blast kick, adds smoke and fire, then rebuilds the ship (scar rims show).
- Pieces bounce on rock, splash and float or sink, and fade when the 2D debris expires.
- Meshes are pre-fractured by part (the parts list is the fracture pattern). Blasts add plank and iron chunks as particles.
- A "hanging" damaged part is a pose, not a joint.

Gameplay consequences already exist: a lost engine means less thrust, a lost bag means less lift, a lost coal bunker starves the boiler. The view reads the per-engine throttles to spin each prop.

## 5. Switching the TV to 3D
- New folder `public/modules/view3d/`; the test page's files move there and grow. `createView3D({canvas, hud, state, camera2d})` follows the same `renderFrame` contract.
- `host.html` gets a `<canvas id="c3d">` under the 2D canvas. The 2D canvas draws only the HUD, arrows and full-screen cards.
- Choosing the view:
  - `?view=2d|3d`, then `localStorage`, then a quality probe (built in WP14: section 17);
  - the pause menu gets "View: 3D/2D";
  - any WebGL failure falls back to 2D.
- Camera:
  - `camera.js` stays the authority; a perspective camera maps the gameplay plane 1:1, at elevation 0.12 rad;
  - HUD anchors use `worldToScreen`;
  - cinematic moments, never with roll: a come-about dolly, a breach pull-back, a finale orbit;
  - the Versus porthole becomes a render target.
- The build page gets a 3D toggle with orbit; the blueprint stays 2D ink.
- Phones are unchanged.
- Everything stays offline: `public/vendor/` is committed, and `start.bat` is untouched.

## 6. Performance
**Target (Medium tier, Intel UHD 620 class, 1080p):** 60 fps with 2 ships, the Kraken and 200 particles; at most 120 draw calls, 350k triangles and 4 ms view JS.

**Tiers:**

| Tier | What it adds or drops |
|---|---|
| High | spot shadows, 6 lanterns, god rays, more ink, shadows 4096 |
| Medium | the target above |
| Low | plain lit, no shadows, LUT only, pixel ratio 0.75 |
| 2D | today's renderer |

**Auto-detect:** probe the GPU, then the existing `perf.js` governor steps down the tiers.

**Perf gate:** `--check-3d` fails above 150 draw calls, 450k triangles or 6 ms view JS.

## 10. WP2 notes: ship materials and hull v2 (built)
**Engine sizes** (SHIP_BUILDING.md b6c): `parts3d/engines.js buildEngine` reads `e.size` through `host/engineArt.js artOf` (a heavy nacelle is 1.35x long with brass bands, twin stacks and a four-bladed propeller; the great lift engine a dark turbine drum, a brass duct ring, stays and a twelve-blade twin rotor); the dyn record carries `ex` (where the exhaust leaves) and `puff` (how big its puffs are: `shipMesh.js` passes them on, `vfx.js` reads them; a starved big engine puffs half as often); the WP6 damage states and the prop spin are unchanged. `tools/view3d-check.mjs` (models) builds a heavy-engine ship, a great-lift ship and four generated ships with sizes and requires empty fallbacks.

**Files** (`public/modules/view3d/`): `textures.js` (the 2048² trim sheet), `parts3d/kit.js` (pieces, trims, the assembler), `parts3d/registry.js` (kind -> builder), `hull.js`, `bag.js`, `decks.js`, `rooms.js`, `stations.js`, `weapons.js`, `engines.js`. `shipMesh.js` is now only the orchestrator and the per-frame `update`.

**Trim sheet.** Painted with Canvas 2D on the first ship (about 13 ms; the old `art/textures/*.png` are blended in as a faint grain when they load). The rows are modulators (near white, grey), the vertex colours carry the hue, so the enemy theme reuses it. Rects: `TRIM` (pixels), `uvRect(name)`, `uvAt(name, tx, ty)`, `plankLayout(name)` (where the boards and butt joints are). WP3/WP4 already have their rows: `rock0-2`, `foam`, `cloud0-7`, `fire0-3`, `smoke`, `scorch0-2`, `hole0-3` (all transparent where they should be). Use `getTrimSheet().texture` (one texture, anisotropy 4, mipmaps). Do not paint a second sheet.

**Registry and keys (what WP5 needs).** `REGISTRY[kind].build(part, ctx, arg) -> { key, batches, dyn, bounds }` for every kind in `shipBuild.PARTS` (data-only kinds have `art: false`). Part ids: `hull`, `deck:<id>`, `room:<name>`, `<type>:<n>` for ladders/ropes/stairs/lifts/poles, `station:<name>`, `gun:<name>`, `searchlight:<name>`, `engine:<name>`, `sail:<name>`, `cannon:<name>`, `gasbag:bagN`, `pipe:*`, `rack:*`, `dropHatch:<name>`, `armour:*`, `ramProw`, `coil`, `medbay` ... The assembler (`kit.assemble`) merges every part's pieces into ONE geometry (`model.assembled`) and keeps `model.ranges[key] = [{ layer, start, count }]` (vertex ranges; the geometry holds each piece twice: the picture and its ink shell, so a ship is one draw call). `model.parts.get(key) = { key, kind, name, deck, x, x0, x1, dyn, bounds }` says what a part is and where. To break a part off: `model.extractPart(key)` (a Group of just its triangles, ink included) plus `parts.get(key).dyn` nodes (guns, props, lamps, hatch leaves, the bag's group), then rebuild the ship from the new layout (the view already does that when `layout.version` changes). Plank and canvas uv offsets are seeded from the part key, so a rebuild does not shuffle them.

**Cut-away.** The hull's two walls are the `neg` and `pos` layers; the wall facing the camera is hidden by a shader switch (`uHide`, one value per ship material, set by `model.setView`) in the picture pass and in the shadow pass. No vertex wobble anywhere: the bag swell is still a scale of its group; the prop angle only integrates `state.engines[i].pow` (read only).

**Measured (classic, day, one ship incl. one crewman):** 81 -> 43 draw calls, 87k -> 101k triangles (mesh + ink + shadow pass); 39 k unique triangles; trim sheet 13 ms; a ship build about 40 ms.

## 11. WP3 notes: terrain, water, clouds, dark caves (built)
**Files** (`public/modules/view3d/`): `terrain.js` (rewritten), `water.js` and `clouds.js` (new), `world.js` (ties the stage together), `lights.js` (the darkness), `sky.js` (the seam fix), `style.js`/`quality.js` (3 new kill-switches), `config.LOOK3D.<env>` (`dark`, `cap`, `clouds`, `water`; see the comment above `LOOK3D`).

**Kill-switches:** `?look=nodark` (the old day-lit caves), `?look=noclouds`, `?look=nowater` (the old plain flat water, no foam, shore or splashes). The dev page has buttons for all three.

**The cave seam (found).** The cave backdrop plane used to RECEIVE the key light's shadows. The key's shadow camera is a box (fitted to the ships, snapped to 200 units): outside it there is no shadow, inside it the rock slab and the ship shade the picture, so the backdrop jumped in brightness along the frustum's top / bottom edge. A horizontal line at about 40% of the screen, a step of 20 -> 29 grey levels in one row (measured on `wp1_after_cave.png`); the same shadows also drew the dark blocks and vertical edges on the picture (`wp3_before_cave.png`). Fix: `cavePlane.receiveShadow = false` (the lamps' light still lands on it).

**Terrain.** Same marching-squares footprint as the 2D game (collision silhouette untouched: only DEPTH is displaced, never x/y). Squares near a cut edge are cut into 3x3 little squares (clipped to the outline); far squares are one flat quad each. Relief = two octaves of stable value noise (integer hash of the world position, so a chunk is the same whenever it is built and neighbours join without cracks), fading to a flat slab 200 px from the edges; the lip is bevelled (rolls back 82 units, width 52-90 by hash); strata = the trim sheet's `rock0-2` rows blended per fragment by a continuous strata coordinate (a small shader patch on the toon material, `aRockUv` + `aStrata` attributes); mossy / snowy caps by environment (vertex colour + a small puff, on up-facing edges and tunnel floors); an ink ribbon along every lip that follows the displaced lip; tunnel walls are rows in depth with hashed bulges, shaded as their plane (a wall is seen edge-on, so per-facet normals made a mosaic of toon steps and rim light). The rock has its own 4-step toon gradient with a real dark step (a lamp behind the front face does not shine through it). Open-sky rock still casts no shadows; cave rock can. Budget kept: 2 chunks per frame.

**Dark caves (approach).** The game's `darkTarget` already drives `world.setTod` (dusk, storm, fungal, aether, ...). 3D adds one number per environment: `LOOK3D.<env>.dark.cave`, the darkness a cave map always has (2D `SEARCHLIGHT.DARK.CAVE` is 0 and stays 0: the sim's bots read it; the owner wants 3D caves dark). `lights.apply` takes the effective darkness `n = max(tod.night, cave floor)`, derives a gloom 0..1 (`dark.from`..`dark.to`) and, with it: the hemisphere light goes to `dark.hemi` with a dark-blue sky/ground floor colour, the key light to `dark.key` of itself, the thin rim fades out, the rock keeps a faint dark-blue floor (`fx.uFloor`, so a dark rock still reads as a silhouette), the cave picture's own glow drops. `world.night` = the effective darkness, so the ship's lamps, lanterns, boiler light and searchlights switch on in caves exactly as they do at night. The lamp lights are what you see by. Tiers: Medium has fewer real lights, so each reaches 2.4x further (the decks stay readable); Low has none, so its gloom is halved. Compared with 2D: the same places are dark (dark skies, dusk) plus the caves.

**Water (Sunken Sea).** One opaque plane at the sea level (it still hides the Kraken's segments below the line). Two colour steps by depth (world z), a stepped mirrored-sky gradient on Medium/High, foam lanes from the trim sheet's foam row (alpha cut in two steps) sliding at a constant speed per lane (linear in time), a hazy edge, a shoreline ring (shallow band + foam ribbon) built once per map where the rock meets the water. `world.splashAt(x, y, size, z)` (`water.splashAt`) draws a double ring decal on the surface for the VFX pass (24 rings, 1 s, quantised to 8 fps, linear growth). Colours and speed: `LOOK3D.sea.water`.

**Clouds.** One InstancedMesh (one draw call): clusters of 3-7 discs from the cloud atlas, drifting at a constant speed in the vertex shader and wrapped round the camera far off screen. Most stand behind the gameplay plane (z -1500..-3800), a few in front (z +350..+1100), thin and faded toward the screen's middle so they never cover the ship for long. Per environment `LOOK3D.<env>.clouds { n, front, alpha, tint }`; none in caves.

**Measured (Medium, 1080p headless, `shot3d`).** Chunk build: median 0.5 ms, p95 0.9 ms, max 1.1 ms once warm (the very first chunks, cold JIT, 3-5 ms); 35 chunks = 13-16k triangles (was 2-8k). Water + clouds together: +3 draw calls, +about 200 triangles (the kill-switches, same frame). In a dark cave on High the first ship's searchlight casts a shadow (a second shadow pass, +30-45k triangles); Medium does not. Host Medium before -> after: day 100 -> 102 calls / 91k -> 94k tris, cave 122 -> 119 / 96k -> 103k, Versus 136 -> 141 / 224k -> 227k. Sim gates: `--check-botsim` byte-identical, `--lint` pass (no simulation file touched).

**For WP4 / later.** `world.splashAt` is the splash hook. `terrain.stats` = chunk times. The shore ring and the splash rings are plain decals: WP4's particles can sit on top. `shipMesh.js` line 200 (the flame frame index `Math.floor(t * 8 + fr.x * 0.013) % 4` can go negative or NaN, so `[fi][ti]` throws "Cannot read properties of undefined" once with a fire on a deck, logged as a warning, not an error): left alone (not mine), needs `((fi % 4) + 4) % 4`.

## 12. WP4 notes: the VFX pass, GPU particles (built)
**Files** (`public/modules/view3d/`): `particles.js` (the system), `vfx.js` (the emitters that read the game's state), `config.VFX3D` (caps, rates, colours), small edits in `index.js` (creates it, one `vfx.update` a frame after the ships and the Kraken are placed), `shipMesh.js` (the old three-tongue flame cones only run when `model.flameFallback` is set), `quality.js` / `style.js` (`?look=novfx` kill-switch: the old flame cones and puff balls come back), the dev page (`VFX test (V)` button, a `Particles` toggle, a particle line in the HUD). No simulation file is touched (`--check-botsim` byte-identical, `--lint` pass).

**The system.** One fixed pool in a `Float32Array`, simulated in JS (linear motion, or plain gravity; drag only slows sideways; no noise anywhere), written once a frame into two interleaved instance buffers and uploaded once. Caps by tier (`config.VFX3D.CAP`): High 1500, Medium 800, Low 400; splinter boxes 160 / 90 / 0; Low also has no smoke trails and half the emit rate (`RATE`). When the pool is full new particles are dropped, smoke first (above 82%), then sparks (94%). Draw calls: **2** quad meshes + **1** instanced mesh of small lit boxes (splinters and chips, toon material, constant spin, gravity) = 3 at most.
- *alpha mesh* (smoke, steam, dust, water droplets): the trim sheet's `smoke` disc (rim softened in the shader), tinted by the hemisphere light (`setAmbient`), so it goes dark at night; fresh smoke is lit orange by its fire for a moment (`warm`). It ignores depth, because a ship's bag and decks would otherwise swallow a column rising from a room.
- *additive mesh* (fire, sparks, embers, muzzle star, rings, flashes, fireballs): HDR colours, so the core of a flame crosses the bloom threshold (keep the HDR near 1.7: more and the tone mapping bleaches it pink). Blending is "premultiplied": a flame covers a bright sky a little instead of washing out. Fire = the 4-frame flipbook stepped at 8 fps; the muzzle flash = a 2-frame star (6 rays, then 8 turned half a step); rings grow in steps at 8 fps; sparks are stretched along their velocity; `ball` is a round toon fireball in three flat colour steps with lumps fixed per particle (no animated noise).
- The random numbers are `particles.rnd` (its own seeded generator): the view never draws from `Math.random`, which the simulation shares in the browser.
- Spawns outside the view (the camera's look-at box plus a margin) are skipped.

**Emitters** (`vfx.js`, all read-only): `state.fires` of EVERY ship (flame flipbook sprite that sits on the deck, little rising flames, embers, a smoke column, a warm glow disc that is strong at night); `puffs` (grouped by place and colour and told apart by colour: orange = fireballs + sparks + smoke, + splinters when it is on a hull; gold = sparks; grey = smoke; white = steam; brown = dust + chips; cold = spray + a water ring on the sea); `flashes` (the muzzle star; the flamethrower's glow flashes become little flames); `rings` (impact ring; at `HITSTOP_AT` 190 world units or more also the one-frame bright flash + sparks); `shells` and `bullets` (a tracer streak, smoke trail on High / Medium, a muzzle star where a NEW shell appears, with the sim's own flash de-duplicated); bombs (smoke + embers trail); the boiler (chimney steam by pressure, thicker and darker in overdrive; vent plumes when a valve is open, a hiss above the warning pressure); engines (exhaust by each engine's own `pow`); the Kraken (spray + mist + `world.splashAt` where a segment crosses the sea line, bigger for the mantle, spray and chips where a tentacle grips); burning debris; the lightning coil's bolt (a bright streak between the bolt's points + a flash). The first frame (or a gap over 0.6 s) only learns what is already there, so a resumed tab does not replay old events.

**API for WP5** (`view.vfx` = `createVfx`, and `view.vfx.P` = the particle system; coordinates are the 3D world's: x = the game's x, **y = UP (the game's y negated)**, z toward the viewer, gameplay plane z = 0; sizes in world units):
- `P.burst(kind, x, y, z, n, opts)` with kind `'fire' | 'smoke' | 'steam' | 'dust' | 'spark' | 'ember' | 'drop' | 'ball' | 'flash' | 'ring' | 'muzzle'`. opts: `dir` (radians, 0 = +x, PI/2 = up, the default), `spread` (half-angle, default PI = all round), `speed`, `life`, `size` ([min, max] or a number), `size1` (end size as a multiple), `color` (hex), `vx vy vz` (inherited velocity), `up`, `ay` (acceleration, negative = down), `drag`, `alpha`, `hdr`, `stretch`, `warm`, `area` (scatter), `force` (spawn even off screen). Returns how many were made.
- `P.spawn(kind, x, y, z, opts)` one particle; `P.splinters(x, y, z, n, { kind: 'wood' | 'iron' | 'rock', speed, up, size, vx, vy, dir, spread })`; `P.explosion(x, y, z, size = 1, { wood, smoke })` the prefab (1 = a shell hit, 2-3 = a bomb or a crash); `P.flash`, `P.ring(x, y, z, size, color, life)`, `P.muzzle(x, y, z, angle, size, color)`, `P.hitFlash(x, y, z, size)`; `P.sprite(...)` one-frame immediate quads (call between `P.begin()` and `P.update()`: only the emitters need it).
- For a broken-off part: `P.explosion(x, y, z, 1.4, { wood: true })` at the break, `P.burst('fire', ...)` + `P.burst('smoke', ...)` on the falling piece each frame (the dev `demo` lists in `vfx.js` show the pattern), `P.splinters(...)` where it lands. The pool is shared: cap the calls per frame (a burst stops by itself when the pool is full).

**Measured** (Medium, 1080p, headless Chrome on the RTX 5070 Ti, `shot3d --gl default`): the pool step + buffer write for **800 live particles = 0.02 ms a frame** (1000-frame loop); the emitters + that = about 0.1 ms in the view's own meter (its timer is coarse). Scene draw calls with fire, fighters, a gunship and the test volley: 176 with the particles, 176 with `?look=novfx` (the old cones and balls cost about what the 2 particle calls do). fps stays at the display's limit (about 170) with 800 live particles. Caveats: the dev page's 8 bots put a fire out in a few seconds, so deck-fire screenshots use the `VFX test` fires (they ride the ship like real ones). Lightning, steam and splinters are the least tuned.

**What the player sees.**
- A part that breaks off tears out of the ship as itself: the lost parts' own 3D triangles (guns, boilers, engines with their barrels and props, a bag with its group) plus the hull, deck and room walls inside the hole (`extractClip`: every triangle is CUT at the edge of the hole, both walls kept), one body per `plan.pieces[i]`. It starts with the 2D debris piece's speed and spin plus a kick away from a blast, falls on the 2D gravity, bounces off the rock (the real marching-squares outline as walls), off the hulls (a kinematic compound of `hitRects` + capsules for the bags: its own ship only after `PHYS.OWN_DELAY`), splashes into the sea with a ring (`world.splashAt`) and floats (wood 0.55, canvas 0.4 against water) or sinks (iron 2.6), smokes (burning pieces also throw sparks), and shrinks away when its 2D twin in `state.debris` expires. - The ship is rebuilt from the new layout in the same frame; the hull walls are CARVED where the scars are (the ship's materials discard the hull's two walls inside up to 8 scar rectangles, with a ragged edge that wanders by a hash of the position and a charred rim 16 px wide); bags, rigging and fittings are not carved (as in 2D, where only the baked hull picture is).

Rock scrapes are long scorch smears, rams big holes. Marks on lost parts or inside a new hole are dropped when the ship is rebuilt.
- Gas holes show as a torn slit (canvas flaps, ink edge) on the bag's skin, set on the side that faces the camera (the 2D game's hole is on the belly, which faces away); when a crew member patches it, a tan canvas patch with stitches takes its place and stays.

**Physics (physics.js).** One `World`, gravity `DEBRIS.GRAVITY` px/s^2 (the world is in game pixels, `lengthUnit` 100), a fixed 60 Hz step (at most 4 a frame), stepped only while a body exists; the engine loads in the background 40 frames after the view starts (about 30 ms, plus a throwaway first step so the first real one is cheap) and, if it ever fails to load, the pieces fall on a plain ballistic path with the same gravity. Pieces are `z`-locked (the gameplay plane) with a huge extra inertia about x and y instead of axis locks (locking the rotation axes switches friction off in Rapier: found the hard way). Rock walls are two-sided (a one-sided trimesh lets a fast body through from the back). Collision groups: debris touches the rock and the ships, never other debris. Bodies are made in the order `ships -> piece index`; chunks use a seeded generator (`ship id + note time`). `tools/physics-check.mjs` proves: the same scripted scenario twice gives the same hash after 600 steps, no NaN, a piece lands on the rock outline and sleeps on it, a piece rides a moving kinematic hull, wood floats and iron sinks, the cap holds; and in a headless browser a forced engine break-off and a bomb bay blast make pieces that fall within 2 s of simulated time, are gone after `LIFE`, with 0 console errors.

**Measured (headless Chrome on SwiftShader, so a real GPU laptop is faster; Rapier in Node).** 36 bodies cost 0.03 ms a physics step in Node; in the browser a step with 12-14 bodies and the rock walls is 0.2-0.4 ms (the first step used to cost 28 ms, `warmup()` now spends it at load; the worst frame seen with a blast is 1.5-4.5 ms). The engine loads in the background (about 30 ms on an idle page, 2-3 s of wall time when the page is busy rendering, never blocking a frame: the pieces fall by hand until it is there). Lifting a break-off's pieces out of the model (`extractClip` + `extractPart`) is a one-off 5-16 ms in the frame it happens. Bodies: at most `DEBRIS.MAX` (12) pieces + `CHUNKS.CAP` (24) chunks. Draw calls: a decal mesh pair a ship (2), one puff mesh (1), and one per piece mesh plus its ink (a piece is one mesh, the ink is in the same draw call): a bomb bay blast with 14 bodies adds about 17 calls for the 7 seconds it lives (88 against 77 for one piece). Rock walls: 15-30 quads a chunk, built only when the engine is awake (the first body), dropped 5 seconds after the last body is gone.

**Hooks left for others.**
- WP4: `destruction.hooks = { smoke(x, y, z, {r, life, color, rise}), spark(x, y, z, {n}), splash(x, y, size) }` default to a small instanced puff pool in `destruction.js` (grey spheres that grow and shrink in 8 fps steps, linear motion). - WP8 (Kraken chunks): DONE in WP8 (section 14: `destruction.addExternal`; this paragraph is what was planned). **What the sim says, what the view does.** The sim decides which things are lit (`state.litTargets`, `obj.lit`, `state.dimTargets`: a thing inside a MANNED beam, `power >= 0.6`) and how far a beam reaches (`searchlights[i].reach`, stopped by rock). The view never decides any of it; it draws it:
- **Beam.** One camera-facing ribbon along the lamp's axis (the camera is nearly orthographic and never rolls, so a card along the beam reads as a volume): a radial soft edge with a brighter core, a fall-off along the length, and DUST: a static noise picture (a soft cloud channel and a sparse fleck channel, painted once from a seeded generator) that slides along the beam at a constant `BEAM.SCROLL` units a second. **How it works.**
- *The state.* The page restarts its simulation after every edit (a fresh `state`), but the 3D view is made once. `buildPane.js` gives the view a forwarding `Proxy` as its state and points it at the new simulation each frame (`pane.frame(now, dt, sim.state)`). The ship's own model is rebuilt by the view when `layout.version` changes (`applyBuild` bumps it on every edit; ship 0's layout is one global object, so the version always moves), and WP5/WP6's per-ship memory (hit log, scars, damage) already resets on a rebuilt layout. While 3D is on, the page's live simulation flies the OPEN-sky map (`config.MAPS.FORCE_KIND` during `startLive` only) so there is no rock in the orbit; the switch restarts the live ship once.
- *The ghost* (`buildStage.js buildGhost`). `buildLayout(newParts)`; every part the live layout lacks (matched by kind and plain values, a deck index replaced by the deck's id, so inserting a deck does not make everything "new"; the hull and rooms are never "new") is built by its REGISTRY builder (`REGISTRY[kind].build(part, ctx, arg)` with a throwaway `makeShipContext`); the fixed pieces are merged into one translucent mesh (vertex colours x a green / red tint, no depth test, the near-side copy of ladders picked each frame), the moving ones (barrels, props, the bag's group) are tinted in place, lights, ink shells and glow cones are dropped, and a box of 12 thin bars is drawn round it. The ghost group sits in the live ship's `content` group (re-attached when the model is rebuilt) and is slid by the difference of the two pivots. Cost: 1.5-3 ms for a gun (2 parts). The spot must hold still for `BUILD3D.GHOST_DEBOUNCE` (70 ms) before it is built; the last 6 are cached and thrown away when the layout version moves.
- *Tiers.* The pane runs the game's performance governor (`host/perf.js`; it also honours the pause menu's Detail setting from `localStorage.airshipDetail`) and starts at Medium: High when the machine is quick, Low (no shadows, no bloom, no real lanterns, pixel ratio 0.75) when it is slow. `?tier=low|medium|high` wins.
- *Off.* The 3D switch off (or any failure: no WebGL, a throw) disposes the pane: the view, its controls, the markers and ghosts, the canvas element (a new canvas is made next time: a canvas whose WebGL context was released cannot get another) and the context itself (`forceContextLoss`). Nothing of 3D is even downloaded while the switch is off or while the Blueprint-only view hides the live pane.

Planes close together share one window with a count badge ("x3 FRIENDS"). Windows fade in and out in four stepped shades (no smooth ramp) and have hysteresis (a plane appears 36 px past the edge and goes when it is 24 px back inside), so a plane that hovers at the edge never flickers. Never over the HUD panels (the top 28% and the bottom message bar are kept clear) and never over each other or the spyglass. Budget by tier: High 3, Medium 2, Low 0 (Low keeps the arrows); the 2D view uses the same numbers by its detail level.
- *The spyglass.* While somebody mans the lookout (crow's nest) a larger BRASS lens hangs at the top of the TV (62% across: clear of the logbook panel, the minimap and the nest) showing the sky far ahead of the ship: it starts `SPYGLASS.AHEAD` (7000 px, about 2.7 screens) ahead along her heading. The lookout's phone stick pans it (right is right on the TV, up is up; critically damped, it never overshoots), within 14000 px ahead, 3500 astern and 3600 up and down. Rings mark every target in the lens, with a cross-hair and a plate ("AHEAD 700 m, Sam ON LOOKOUT - 1 SPOTTED"). A target held in the lens for 0.5 s is SPOTTED exactly as if the lookout had tapped it on the radar (`spotter.markSpot`: the TV marks it, shells hurt it 25% more, the phone pings "SPOTTED FIGHTER!", `spotRecent` gives the LOOKOUT <-> HELM +25%, the lookout arrows and the spotted arrows show it); the mark is kept up quietly while it stays in the lens. In a dark sky only what a searchlight holds can be seen (`SPYGLASS.DARK`), and the picture is as dark as the sky. Nobody in the nest: the lens fades away (and forgets its place after 4 s). Not in Versus (the camera there already shows both ships and has its own far-ship porthole, untouched). The phone's hint now reads "PAN THE SPYGLASS: the stick looks far ahead (top of the TV). Spot what comes!".
- *Bots.* A bot lookout pans toward the nearest unspotted threat ahead (`botTarget`) and spots it like a human (`config.SPYGLASS.BOTS`).

**Files.**
- `host/portholes.js` (new, Node-safe): the planner. `plan({ view, w, h, dt, tier, mode3d }) -> { items, spy }`: edge placement, hysteresis, clusters, budget, stepped fades, the spyglass item with its marks.

**How a 3D porthole is drawn.** A small extra draw of the SAME scene from a camera of its own (`placeCamera`, the plain gameplay lens, so nothing rolls) into a square viewport of the canvas through its own little composer; its last pass discards the corners. Sky, clouds and sea move to its place for the pass (`world.update`; the next frame's main pass moves them back); the key light's shadow map is not redrawn (`shadowMap.autoUpdate` off: the planes carry no shadow there); no particles (the main view culls them to its own screen), no beams, and the edge windows run without bloom. If anything throws, 3D portholes switch themselves off and the 2D renderer draws them. `renderer.autoClear` is off for these passes (an auto clear blackened the corners of the square).

**Escorts in 3D (two bugs the owner reported, fixed in `view3d/flyers.js`).** (1) Escorts are per ship (`ship.ctx.escorts`), but the flyers drew only the world state's list, so in Versus the second ship's fighters were invisible. The friendly-porthole selection reads the same lists: planes of the main ship's team only. (2) Docked escorts were not drawn at all. Now a docked escort is the plane model sitting on its hook under the hull, placed with `api.shipPoint` (the ship's own layout point mapped through her model, so it follows her pose, pitch and mirrored facing; the nose follows a second point 100 px forward), and a plane under rebuild (`rebuild > 0`) is a see-through ghost (cloned materials at 35%). Returning planes are flying planes until they land, so they were already drawn. `flyers.stats()` now reports `docked`, `ghosts` and `friends`.

**Simulation impact.** `state.spy` is a new world key written only by `spyglass.js`; nothing reads it but the TV. Spotting by the spyglass is the only thing that reaches the simulation (it draws no random numbers). `config.SPYGLASS.BOTS` (default on) makes bot lookouts pan and spot; `tools/botsim.mjs` and `tools/voyagesim.mjs` set it off unless `WITH_SPYGLASS=1`, so every baseline stays what it was (`--check-botsim` 9/9 byte-identical).

## 28. R1 notes: the ring bend and the ring camera (built)
LOOP.md package R1: a ring map (`map.ring`: tower, fortress, citadel; `host.html?view=3d&kind=ring&variant=tower|fortress|citadel`) is drawn as a circle round its castle. Plain words: the 2D game still plays on a flat strip; the 3D picture bends that strip into a ring round the castle, so the ship seems to circle it. No simulation file is touched.

**Files** (`public/modules/view3d/`): `ringBend.js` (new: the maths in JS and GLSL, the Three.js chunk patches, the shared uniforms, the frustum-culling hook), `ringCastle.js` (new: the placeholder castle), `index.js` (`ringFrame` once a frame, `hud3d.project` / `mapPt`, `hudView` -> `view.scr`, `alignError`, `stats().ring`, the castle), `style.js` (`paintedPlane` gets `RING_SKIP`), `particles.js`, `lines3d.js`, `contactShadow.js`, `beams.js` (their vertex shaders bend a world point), `water.js` (the sea plane is `RING_SKIP` and reaches back past the castle), `scenery.js` (a revolving gun is drawn at the ring image nearest the camera), `portholes.js` (`RB.pass`: each window bends round its own look-at point); host side: `threatArt.js`, `spotterArt.js`, `searchlightArt.js` (far markers through `view.scr`). `config.RING3D`. Tools: `tools/ring3d-check.mjs`, `cine3d-check.mjs` (step `ring`), `view3d-check.mjs` (scene `ring`), `buildsim --check-ring3d`.

**The path.** The strip's x is arc length. Before the ring: a straight line, then (the last `LEAD` * R px, 0.8 R) an arc of half the ring's curvature, then the circle R * (sin t, cos t), t = (x - xRing) / R (ring.js `ringAngle`); the castle's axis is the origin. A point (x, y, z) is P(x) + z * normal(x), so z = 0 is the plane the game is played in, z > 0 is outward (toward the viewer at the ship). Periodic: x and x + C land on the same spot, so a revolving gun that wraps (`mx` jumps back by C) is already seamless; the gun is drawn at the image nearest the camera.

**The camera frame.** The camera never moves round the ring. Every frame `RB.setFrame(xc)` rotates and shifts the path so that the point under the look-at (x = xc) sits at (xc, 0) heading +x. `ringBend()` (GLSL, the same in `RB.pt` for the HUD) maps a flat point into that frame. Within `flat` px of xc (the ship's half length + 200, 700 to 1500) the strip is left perfectly flat and then eased (`smoothstep`, `BLEND` 900 px) into the true circle: the ship is rigid, her anchors are exact, and beyond that the ring is the true circle. The camera, the sheared lens, the sky strips, the clouds, the culling windows, the shadow fit, the cinematics (a yaw about the look-at composes with it) are all as they were.

**How it reaches every shader.** At import `ringBend.js` patches the Three.js chunks `project_vertex`, `worldpos_vertex` (shadow lookups), `defaultnormal_vertex` (the normal turns with the strip) and `logdepthbuf_pars_vertex` (the declarations), and wraps `THREE.UniformsUtils.clone` so every built-in program gets the SAME four uniform objects (`uRingA..D`); one assignment a frame reaches all of them. When `uRingA.x` is 0 (no ring) the branch is not taken: nothing changes. A material with `defines: { RING_SKIP: '' }` is left alone (painted sky planes, the sea, the keep). A custom `ShaderMaterial` that places things from world points must include `RING_GLSL`, call `RB.addUniforms(uniforms)` and pass its world position through `ringBend()` (particles, lines, contact shadow and beams do). THE RULE FOR NEW 3D CODE: put things in the flat map frame as always; if you project a point yourself, use `V.hud3d.project` (it bends); if you write a vertex shader that does not use the chunks, bend the world point.
- Frustum culling asks about the FLAT bounding sphere, which the bend moves, so `ringBend.js` replaces `Frustum.prototype.intersectsObject` (only while a ring is on, and not for the post-processing quads, which have no parent): it tests the BENT centre with a radius of 1.5 x + 100. `?ring=nocull` draws everything (dev).
- The terrain is NOT rebuilt or bent on the CPU: it stays flat in `terrain.js` (so the stable-hash rock paint, the chunk streaming, the Rapier trimesh and the wind sway are all as before) and the vertex shader bends it each frame; the cost is a few extra instructions a vertex.

**The HUD.** `hud3d.project` bends the point; `hudView(view, w, h)` returns the view with `scr(x, y, w, h)` (a map point, y down, to canvas pixels through the bend) and `ring: true` on a ring map; the 2D HUD's far markers use `view.scr` when it is there. `V.alignError(view, w, h[, whole])` on a ring measures the grid over the flat zone (the part that must be 1:1); `whole = 1` measures the whole screen (it grows toward the edges: 13 px on the fortress, 26 px on the tower). Round every angle of all three rings, the approach and a COME ABOUT (cinematics off): 0.0000 px on the grid and on six anchors of the ship, no roll; with the cinematics on the COME ABOUT dolly composes (the keep swings up to 0.22 of the screen width off the middle while the camera yaws 10 degrees round the ship, then it is back and exact).

**The placeholder castle** (`ringCastle.js`, R2 replaces it). Faces: a stone block at each face (880 x 760 like the 2D wall, battlements) with a dark alcove, the CORE glowing in it behind a SHUTTER plate that slides up in four steps while `face.open > 0`, a lamp (red shut, green blinking open), the specials (hangar shed, flak, mortar), the gallery's ledge with its braces, rail and door; a breached face is a heap of rubble. The Flagship's MOORING ropes (citadel) and the gallery's zip-lines are lines. The keep: a tower (`RING3D.KEEP`: 0.2 R wide, 2600 px above the platforms) at `RB.axis()`. The faces' stone is one merged mesh plus an ink shell, rebuilt only when a face falls, a special is wrecked or a shutter moves a step; the keep is another; four small glow meshes. About 12 draw calls, 0.1 ms.

**Measured** (headless Chrome, RTX 5070 Ti, `--gl default`, Medium, 1600x900, 8 bots flying each ring live; the Medium budgets are 150 calls, 450k triangles, 6 ms): tower 122 calls / 158k tris / view JS 0.9 ms (1.2 max); fortress 100 / 140k / 0.9 (2.3 max); citadel 75 / 125k / 0.7 (1.2 max); about 95 to 100 fps; `ringFrame` (the frame set-up and the castle) about 0.1 ms. The ring scene's fixture is `tools/fixtures/shots3d/ring.png`.

**Gates.** `node tools/buildsim.mjs --check-ring3d [--quick] [--shots DIR] [--only scenes,parity,circle,comeabout,low,spy,twod,shots] [--variants ...] [--env storm|sea|ember|...]` (own server, stopped by PID): see the header of `tools/ring3d-check.mjs`. `parity` reads the GLSL bend back from a float render target and compares it with `RB.pt` (the HUD's): worst 0.27 world units (a quarter of a pixel at most; that is this GPU's sin / cos precision, float32 arithmetic alone gives 0.003). `--shots DIR` writes the ship circling the tower and the fortress (0 / 90 / 180 / 270 degrees, clear air picked near each) and the citadel with the moored Flagship, close and wide.

**Known limits.** One lap only (the bend is periodic, but nothing is built round the far side beyond the terrain window of about 7200 px); the keep is not on the strip, so it has no shadow on the faces; the beams and the lit-target rim are bent but only tested near the ship; Rapier wreckage stays flat (it falls on the flat rock, which is drawn bent, so it matches); the sea on a ring reaches back R + 3000 px (the shore ring is for the flat map's rock only).

## 39. The Island Titan in 3D (built)
**Files** (`public/modules/view3d/`): `creatureTitan.js` (the Titan's extension of the generic creature view: it places every piece from the sim's parts each frame; the stepped head, jaw, legs, kneel and crush; the stone it sheds; the death; the shelled-off arm's slabs), `titanModel.js` (the torso with its heartstone and vein, the rubble heap, the legs, head, jaw, island, trees, ruin, crystal in four stages and the boulder, plus the stone kit: jittered icosahedra, cut blocks, rings, box-mapped stone UVs), `titanArms.js` (the slabs, hands and the cut face), `titanPaint.js` (the tiling weathered stone and its glow twin, painted with Canvas 2D), `titanFx.js` (the marks: one draw call). **What it looks like.** A slow stone colossus in soft faded daylight colours (warm grey stone, moss, a cyan crystal, amber eyes), all flat-faceted rock with thin ink:
- BODY: a heavy faceted torso with broad shoulders, shoulder mounds, a ridge of back rocks, chest plates and moss caps (a dark, warm grey so the lighter head and arms read against it). The HEARTSTONE (a cyan cut diamond in a stone ring) sits in the chest and a glowing VEIN of crystal studs climbs the chest toward the island (a two-key pulse on the heartstone, a steady gentle glow on the vein). The stone texture is a tiling painting (speckle, faint strata, cracks); a few of its cracks are painted cyan in a glow twin (the toon material's emissive map), so the whole colossus shows thin glowing veins.
- HEAD: a blocky skull in lighter stone with a heavy dark brow, a nose bridge, ear stones, moss, big glowing amber eyes (glow channel 1) and a wide mouth. The skull's lower front is cut away: under the upper lip (with its row of teeth and the dark red throat behind) there is only the hinged JAW, so a dropped jaw opens a gap that reads from the side. It tips back in three held steps for the roar, the jaw opens in sixths (`openAmt`) and the throat glows (channel 2); a ring and dust leave the mouth as it opens.
- ARMS: for each sim arm three stone SLABS placed by the sim's own segments (position, angle, a tilt in depth from the shoulder to the hand, flipped so the moss stays on top), each a faceted block with a ball joint, a darker band and moss. The hand is OPEN (four stone fingers and a thumb), a FIST (knuckles round a held boulder or the gasbag) or BROKEN (two stubs). A hurt arm cracks at 66% and 33% of its health: the far slabs get a bite out of them with cyan light in the cracks, the hand breaks, and stone chips fall off as Rapier bodies with dust and sparks. A hand that grips the gasbag is a fist at the ship's depth (`Z.GRIP`), so it lies on the bag's side.
- LEGS: two long stone legs with a knee, a mossy patch and a foot block with three toes; while it walks they swap and lift on the sim's 8 a second key (`cr.titan.leg`, `walking`): a lift of `LEGS.LIFT` with a swing about the hip, and a puff of dust and a ring where a foot comes down. The rubble heap it takes its boulders from lies in front of the feet.
- ISLAND: a grassy rock slab on its back: a flat grass top, an earth band, hanging stone and roots, tufts of grass along the lip, two round trees and a ruined arch (stone pillars and a lintel).
